**1. Artificial hallucinations in ChatGPT: Implications in scientific writing**
Alkaissi, H., & McFarlane, S. I., 2023, Cureus
[https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.35179](https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.35179)
Documents early case-based evidence of ChatGPT generating fabricated clinical references that are stylistically correct but point to nonexistent articles.

**2. Uniting the tribes of fluency to form a metacognitive nation**
Alter, A. L., & Oppenheimer, D. M., 2009, Personality and Social Psychology Review
[https://doi.org/10.1177/1088868309341564](https://doi.org/10.1177/1088868309341564)
Synthesizes decades of manipulation studies showing that cognitive fluency functions as a metacognitive cue that people use to judge truth independent of content.

**3. Online searches to evaluate misinformation can increase its perceived veracity**
Aslett, K., et al., 2023, Nature
[https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06883-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06883-y)
Shows that searching online to verify false claims can perversely increase belief by surfacing low-quality corroborating sources in data voids.

**4. Exploring the boundaries of reality: Investigating the phenomenon of artificial intelligence hallucination...**
Athaluri, S. A., et al., 2023, Cureus
[https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.37432](https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.37432)
Revealed that 69% of references generated by ChatGPT for research proposals could not be verified as genuine.

**5. Assessing the performance of 8 AI chatbots in bibliographic reference retrieval...**
Cabezas-Clavijo, Á., & Sidorenko-Bautista, P., 2025, arXiv
[https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18059](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18059)
Evaluates eight consumer chatbots, revealing that nearly 40% of generated citations were erroneous or entirely invented.

**6. Heuristic versus systematic information processing and the use of source versus message cues in persuasion**
Chaiken, S., 1980, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology
[https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.39.5.752](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.39.5.752)
Foundational research establishing that low-involvement message recipients rely heavily on peripheral cues rather than argument quality.

**7. Hallucination rates and reference accuracy of ChatGPT and Bard for systematic reviews**
Chelli, M., et al., 2024, Journal of Medical Internet Research
[https://doi.org/10.2196/53164](https://doi.org/10.2196/53164)
Found hallucination rates reached up to 91.4% for Bard and 39.6% for GPT-3.5 when attempting to reproduce reference lists.

**8. Large legal fictions: Profiling legal hallucinations in large language models**
Dahl, M., et al., 2024, Journal of Legal Analysis
[https://doi.org/10.1093/jla/laae003](https://doi.org/10.1093/jla/laae003)
Found hallucination rates of at least 58% for ChatGPT-4 on verifiable questions about federal court cases.

**9. Starstruck by journal prestige and citation counts?**
Eika, E., & Sandnes, F. E., 2022, Scientometrics
[https://doi.org/10.1007/s11192-022-04521-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11192-022-04521-4)
Demonstrates that student readers judge publication trustworthiness heavily based on surface-level reference attributes.

**10. Knowledge does not protect against illusory truth**
Fazio, L. K., et al., 2015, Journal of Experimental Psychology: General
[https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000098](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000098)
Shows that repetition-driven fluency can override prior factual knowledge when individuals judge the validity of statements.

**11. Frequency and the conference of referential validity**
Hasher, L., et al., 1977, Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior
[https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-5371(77)80012-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-5371(77)80012-1)
Foundational study documenting the illusory truth effect where statements are rated higher for validity on repeated encounters.

**12. Authority, truth, and citation bias: A large-scale multi-domain benchmark...**
Khurana, A., et al., 2026, arXiv
[https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13104)
Shows that LLMs themselves are more likely to accept false claims when a formatted citation is attached to the prompt.

**13. Who checks the citations? Benchmarking legal hallucination detection**
Liu, P., et al., 2026, arXiv
[https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21155](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21155)
Catalogs over a thousand instances of AI-fabricated citations in legal filings and discusses the necessity of adversarial checks.

**14. Hallucination-free? Assessing the reliability of leading AI legal research tools**
Magesh, V., et al., 2025, Journal of Empirical Legal Studies
[https://doi.org/10.1111/jels.12413](https://doi.org/10.1111/jels.12413)
Found that leading proprietary legal AI products hallucinated in 17% to 33% of queries despite utilizing retrieval architecture.

**15. Seeing is believing: The effect of brain images on judgments of scientific reasoning**
McCabe, D. P., & Castel, A. D., 2008, Cognition
[https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2007.07.017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2007.07.017)
Foundational study establishing the "seductive allure" effect where logically irrelevant technical detail inflates perceived rigor.

**16. Credibility and trust of information in online environments: The use of cognitive heuristics**
Metzger, M. J., & Flanagin, A. J., 2013, Journal of Pragmatics
[https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pragma.2013.07.012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pragma.2013.07.012)
Synthesizes how readers use cognitive heuristics, such as the presence of a citation, for rapid credibility judgments.

**17. On the (non)persuasive power of a brain image**
Michael, R. B., et al., 2013, Psychonomic Bulletin & Review
[https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-013-0391-6](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-013-0391-6)
Large-scale replication indicating the persuasive effect of irrelevant technical detail is measurable but smaller than previously thought.

**18. Source-credibility information and social norms improve truth discernment...**
Prike, T., et al., 2024, Scientific Reports
[https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-57560-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-57560-7)
Found that a dynamically updated source-credibility badge increased belief in true claims and decreased engagement with misinformation.

**19. A survey on automatic credibility assessment using textual credibility signals...**
Srba, I., et al., 2024, arXiv
[https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2410.21360](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2410.21360)
Reviews how explicit credibility signals and citations function as heuristics in the era of large language models.

**20. Fabrication and errors in the bibliographic citations generated by ChatGPT**
Walters, W. H., & Wilder, E. I., 2023, Scientific Reports
[https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-41032-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-41032-5)
Audit revealing that 55% of GPT-3.5's citations and 18% of GPT-4's citations across 42 topics were entirely fabricated.
